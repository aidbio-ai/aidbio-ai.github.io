AidBio did not begin with AI.

It began with a biological question: how do interacting signals become an observable phenotype? Two decades later, the same question sits underneath AidBio — now expressed through bioinformatics, machine learning, scientific software, environmental data and AI-assisted research.

The founder thesis

The tools changed. The scientific method did not.

Mechanism first

Start by identifying variables, interactions, measurements, causal hypotheses and confounders. This came directly from molecular genetics and experimental plant biology.

Represent complexity

When biological relationships become too dense for intuition alone, convert them into structured data: expression matrices, interaction graphs, phenotypes and environmental time series.

Make evidence reusable

A one-off analysis answers one experiment. Software encodes a repeatable way of answering a class of scientific questions.

2005 → today

From crop biotechnology to scientific AI

The story begins in applied agricultural biotechnology at Alellyx, deepens through doctoral and postdoctoral research on sugar, hormone and stress signalling, expands into protein interaction networks and crop biology, then moves increasingly into computation, graph learning, climate analytics and agentic research software.

The important point is continuity: the computational phase was not an escape from biology. It was a response to biological complexity.
