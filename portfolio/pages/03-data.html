Chapter 03 · Data

A network across genotypes, sugars and circadian time is not a spreadsheet you can eyeball.

Once AtbZIP63 was tied back to the circadian clock and to plant growth, the experiments needed to span many genotypes, treatments and timepoints at once — the point where biology became a high-dimensional data problem rather than a bench problem.

The question changed shape

Does the same transcription factor shape both the clock and growth?

Follow-up work asked whether AtbZIP63, already known to integrate sugar and ABA signals, also feeds into circadian clock timing and, through it, plant growth. Answering that required comparing wild-type and mutant lines across sugar treatments and time-of-day sampling — growth curves, circadian period estimates and gene-expression time series layered on top of each other.

No single measurement answers that question. It only becomes visible once the different data layers are aligned, normalized and analyzed together.

What the data layers were

Three data types, one underlying regulator

GenotypesWild type vs. bzip63 mutant lines compared side by side
TreatmentsSugar availability manipulated across conditions
Time seriesCircadian-phase sampling to estimate clock period and growth dynamics

Co-authored research article · peer-reviewed · open in a new tab

Measured result: genotype, treatment and time-of-day jointly shape growth and clock phenotypes in this dataset. Interpretation: bZIP63 is one point where sugar status is translated into circadian and growth output — this study does not claim it is the only such point.

Multi-layered data does not interpret itself. It needs models built specifically to respect its structure.

That need — for models that reason over structured, high-dimensional biological data rather than flatten it — is exactly what pushed the work toward computational and graph-based prediction.
