Chapter 02 · Networks

One node is never the whole story. Signals converge on hubs.

Postdoctoral work moved the question up a level: instead of one transcription factor, how do carbon, nitrogen, calcium and stress signals share regulatory hubs across a whole network?

The shift in scale

From "this gene responds" to "this hub is shared"

At ITQB NOVA, research on calcium-dependent protein kinases (CDPKs) asked a systems-level question: carbon/nitrogen metabolism and abiotic-stress responses are usually studied as separate regulatory networks, but they converge on overlapping signalling components. CDPKs — calcium sensors that decode stress and nutrient status — were examined as a candidate "missing link" connecting the two networks.

This is a genuinely different kind of claim than a single-gene mechanism: it is a synthesis across many published experiments, organized to expose where regulatory network topology, not just individual gene function, does the explanatory work.

Distinguishing the claim types

A review synthesizes; it does not re-run the experiments

Evidence base

Synthesizes experimentally supported CDPK targets and interactions reported across the carbon/ nitrogen-metabolism and stress-response literature.

Interpretation

Proposes CDPKs as a candidate structural link between two networks usually studied in isolation — a hypothesis for further experimental network mapping, not a newly measured interactome.

Why it matters here

This is the first time in the story that the unit of analysis becomes the network itself, rather than a gene — the conceptual seed for AidBio's later graph-based tools.
