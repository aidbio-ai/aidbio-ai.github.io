Chapter 01 · Learn the mechanism

Before prediction, understand regulation.

The founder story begins with Arabidopsis and a transcription factor called bZIP63. The central lesson was not a specific gene; it was how to think about interacting signals.

01 Mechanism02 Networks03 Data04 Models05 Software06 AidBio
Doctoral research · UNICAMP

A gene is rarely an isolated switch.

The 2011 Plant Physiology study established AtbZIP63 as a sensitive integrator of transient glucose and abscisic-acid (ABA) signals. In practical terms, cellular energy status and stress-hormone signalling converge on transcriptional regulation rather than acting as unrelated inputs.

This establishes the intellectual template that later becomes computational: identify the inputs, the integration point, the downstream outputs and the conditions under which the relationship changes.

Tutorial takeaway. Ask six questions: What are the variables? Which interact? What is directly measured? What is inferred? What is plausibly causal? What could confound the result?

Circadian entrainment by sugars

The study showed that bZIP63 regulates the clock gene PRR7, and that sugar availability can adjust circadian phase through a pathway involving Tre6P, SnRK1/KIN10 and bZIP63.

Why this matters to the AidBio story

A regulatory mechanism had become a dynamic system:

1 light drives photosynthesis
2 photosynthesis changes sugar availability
3 sugar/energy signalling regulates bZIP63
4 bZIP63 regulates PRR7
5 the circadian phase changes
6 timing affects metabolism and growth

The biological problem now spans molecules, time and phenotype — exactly the kind of multidimensional structure that eventually demands computational representation.

The same mechanism scales toward growth.

The 2021 New Phytologist paper showed that bZIP63 mutants mismanage night-time starch use and display photoperiod-dependent growth impairment. In short-day conditions, mutant rosette area and dry weight were substantially reduced, linking metabolic timing to a visible phenotype.

NEW PHYTOLOGIST · 2021 · CC BY

bZIP63 modulates plant growth

Energy status ↔ circadian regulation ↔ starch breakdown ↔ growth.

Viana AJC†, Matiolli CC†, et al. The sugar-responsive circadian clock regulator bZIP63 modulates plant growth. New Phytologist 231, 1875–1889 (2021). DOI: 10.1111/nph.17518.
