# Evidence map

Every scientific claim used in the six-chapter founder story links back to a
primary source below. "Measured result" statements are taken from the
abstract/summary of the cited paper; "interpretation" statements are
explicitly labelled as such on the page they appear on.

## Chapter 01 — Mechanism

- Matiolli CC, Tomaz T, Duarte GT, Prado FM, Del Bem LE, Silveira AB, et al.
  (2011). *The Arabidopsis bZIP Gene AtbZIP63 Is a Sensitive Integrator of
  Transient Abscisic Acid and Glucose Signals.* Plant Physiology 157(2),
  692–705.
  - PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21844310/
  - Journal: https://academic.oup.com/plphys/article/157/2/692/6108890
  - Claims used: transient repression of AtbZIP63 by 2% glucose,
    HXK1-independent; ABA-modulated repression at 6% glucose.

## Chapter 02 — Networks

- Alves HLS, Matiolli CC, Soares RC, Almadanim MC, Oliveira MM, Abreu IA
  (2021). *Carbon/nitrogen metabolism and stress response networks —
  calcium-dependent protein kinases as the missing link?* Journal of
  Experimental Botany 72(12), 4190–4201.
  - DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/jxb/erab136
  - Open access: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8162629/
  - Claims used: CDPKs proposed as a candidate structural link between
    carbon/nitrogen metabolism and stress-response signalling networks
    (review/synthesis, not a newly measured interactome — flagged as such
    on the page).

## Chapter 03 — Data

- Viana AJC, Matiolli CC, Newman DW, Vieira JGP, Duarte GT, Martins MCM,
  Gilbault E, Hotta CT, Caldana C, Vincentz M (2021). *The sugar-responsive
  circadian clock regulator bZIP63 modulates plant growth.* New Phytologist
  231(5), 1875–1889.
  - DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.17518
  - Open access: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9292441/
  - Claims used: genotype × sugar-treatment × circadian-time design linking
    bZIP63 to clock period and growth phenotypes.

## Chapter 04 — Models

- Vieira JGP, Duarte GT, Barrera-Rojas CH, Matiolli CC, Viana AJC, Campos
  RA, Canesin LED, Vicentini R, Nogueira FTS, Vincentz M (2024). *Regulation
  of abscisic acid receptor mRNA stability: Involvement of microRNA5628 in
  PYL6 transcript decay.* Plant Physiology 197(1), kiae663.
  - PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39707902/
  - Journal: https://academic.oup.com/plphys/article-abstract/197/1/kiae663/7930158
  - Claims used: miR5628 identified as a candidate regulator of PYL6
    transcript decay, supported by decay-assay evidence involving XRN4.
